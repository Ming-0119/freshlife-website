Our Philosophy

We believe eating at home
deserves to feel easier.

This page explains what FreshLife believes, what it does today, and where it plans to go next. The vision stays grounded: future ideas are never presented as features that already exist.

Last updated: 11 September 2026Kept in sync with the feature list and roadmap

What we believe

  • Family food decisions shouldn’t run on guesswork. “What do we have, what’s for dinner, what’s about to expire, what’s missing, what to buy” — these small daily decisions shouldn’t take up so much energy.
  • Answers should start from what your home actually has. A generic tip is never as useful as one that looks at your fridge and pantry first.
  • Privacy is the foundation, not a feature. What your family eats doesn’t need to be uploaded anywhere. Core features running on-device is FreshLife’s default — not an option you have to unlock.
  • AI is an assistant, not the decision-maker. It can suggest; you and your family decide. Important changes always need your confirmation first.
  • Honesty is the best long-term strategy. What works today and what’s planned are both written down clearly — launch status is never inflated, and plans are never dressed up as facts.

What we’re doing today

Current releases & iOS preview

FreshLife is a local-first food management tool for your household. Android and native HarmonyOS are released with their respective local pantry and shopping features; the web app is also available. iOS is preparing for release. The following is an iOS feature preview, not a promise of the same capabilities on every platform:

  • Pantry & expiry reminders: see what you have, where it is stored and how long it keeps, with local iOS reminders before an item is due.
  • Four ways to add food: snap, album, scan or type. Recognition runs on-device, no API key.
  • Meals & cooking: meal ideas based on what is already at home, with near-date ingredients prioritized and step-by-step timers while you cook.
  • Shopping list: keep missing ingredients in one place, then confirm what you bought before adding it to your pantry.
  • iPhone and iPad, no account needed; export or delete your data anytime.

Every item on the All Features page follows “what it helps with — how to use it — what to watch for — current status,” and matches the app’s actual version.

What comes next

Next · Not shipped yet

These directions build on existing local features, step by step. The complete capabilities are not yet available:

  1. Easier food entry. Make recording food faster and easier: batch photo import, smarter recognition checks, templates for your usual buys.
  2. Household rhythm & personal context. Learn your household’s tastes, dietary restrictions and usual buys on-device, so suggestions fit better over time.
  3. Better planning & restocking. Move from planning tonight’s meal to seeing how the whole week fits together, with weekly meal plans and restock suggestions based on real use.

Before anything ships, we’ll explain the change here and inside the app. Memory-type abilities stay on-device, off by default, explained first and enabled by you. Timing depends on accounts, service review and compliance processes.

Where we want to go in the long run

Long-term direction · Where we’re headed

FreshLife aims to become a personal food intelligence layer for each household: understanding over time what your family keeps at home, enjoys eating and tends to buy, so everyday food decisions take less effort.

In practice, that could mean understanding your household’s tastes, dietary restrictions, regular purchases and changing routines, then offering a useful suggestion at the right moment. It would still leave every decision with you; its role is to reduce the effort around that decision.

To be clear: this is a long-term direction, not a feature available today. Version 1.0 has no long-term household memory, automatic shopping, complete household food graph or automatic entry across shopping channels. Those capabilities depend on accounts, compliance and sustained product work. They will follow the roadmap in stages, with an honest update before each step ships.

Boundaries: what we commit not to do

  • Never present suggestions as professional advice. AI suggestions are for general reference only—not medical, nutritional or food-safety advice. For important decisions, follow product labels and qualified professionals.
  • No unverifiable promises. We won’t claim specific savings, waste-reduction percentages or rankings; we describe what the features do.
  • No data collection beyond what a feature needs. Optional remote AI (if it ever ships) stays off by default, needs clear consent, and sends only the minimum for each request.
  • No silent memory features. Existing local records remain under your control. Any new online memory service will explain its scope and request the necessary consent.

Three things people get wrong

“FreshLife will make my decisions for me?”

No. It organizes information and offers suggestions; decisions stay with you and your family. Important pantry changes always need your confirmation.

“Is it quietly learning about me in the background?”

Cloud memory is not enabled silently. Android 1.0.1 includes local household settings and estimates based on confirmed records. Full cloud household memory is not available yet.

“It will shop for me automatically?”

No. Automatic shopping is neither a current feature nor a near-term direction. The shopping list reminds you what’s missing; what you buy is your call.

Waste reduction & circularity · In design

A narrow scope. A clear destination.

FreshLife plans to connect food use, separated leftovers and material destinations. An interactive design exists for all three platforms, but recovery records are not in the released apps. We do not currently accept materials, bookings, deliveries, collection requests or redeemable reward claims. Local records and official guidance come first; a physical pilot requires confirmed recipients and processing routes.

The planned app may record general disposal or an unknown destination. A record does not mean FreshLife accepts the material. The boundaries below concern the proposed physical pilot.

Possible pilot · Recipient required

Separated tea leaves and coffee grounds

Explore these two streams at designated communities or shops. Acceptance depends on a processor’s stated composition, contamination, moisture and delivery requirements. Tea bags, filters, capsules and containers are not automatically included.

Guidance only · No collection

Food packaging accepted locally

Offer region-specific guidance for facilities that explicitly accept the material. Unknown, composite or contaminated packaging must not automatically be labelled recyclable. Separate containers, lids and contents as the facility requires.

Outside the proposed pilot

Mixed food waste and hazardous items

Cooked leftovers, meat and bones, used oil, mixed rubbish, batteries, electronics, medicines, medical waste, chemicals and hazardous sharps are outside this pilot. Flowers, garden waste and other biomass are not included simply because they are organic. Use applicable local channels.

This is a proposed FreshLife service boundary, not a universal recycling rule. Every future campaign must publish an acceptance list. Ask about unlisted materials, do not discard edible food to participate, and do not send materials to FreshLife.

From leftovers to a documented destination

  1. 01 · Prevent waste first

    Check stock before buying and record actual use. Keep packaging, normal residues and discarded food separate, without deducting inventory twice.

  2. 02 · Check eligibility

    Read the campaign’s accepted materials, preparation rules, location, opening times and capacity before arranging delivery.

  3. 03 · Deliver separately

    A pilot would use designated community or shop collection points, not default home pickup. Follow preparation instructions; do not stockpile, mix or post materials.

  4. 04 · Recipient inspection

    The recipient checks material, contamination and net weight. Both parties reference one receipt, with reasons for rejection. Partial acceptance records only the accepted portion.

  5. 05 · Track the batch

    A capable processor with applicable qualifications records handover, processing, outputs and residual disposal. Missing evidence remains “awaiting update”.

  6. 06 · Report and settle

    Show verified batch outcomes and settle eligible campaign benefits. Explain the next use and eventual disposal of new products; one drop-off is not an endless loop.

What would the collected material become?

Eligible tea leaves or coffee grounds could be explored as inputs for regenerated materials, subject to a confirmed processor and specification. Other routes require their own acceptance and processing conditions. No particular item, product or complete recovery is guaranteed.

Each batch would show accepted weight, processor, actual route, material or product use, and the destination of unrecovered residues. Wet, dry and finished-product weights stay distinct. A handover alone is not proof of regeneration. Energy recovery, composting and material regeneration must be described separately.

Any future regenerated product should disclose traceable source material, verifiable content, intended use and its next disposal route. Claims such as suitability for food contact require separate verification. Buying a product would not be required to receive a recovery benefit.

How should participants be rewarded?

Useful feedback first; funded benefits later. Local records would provide personal progress and sorting reviews, with no redeemable points for self-reported check-ins. A partner-funded pilot could offer specified coupons or small gifts. There is no promise of cash returns, stored value, carbon credits or appreciating points.

Proposed flow: recipient-confirmed eligible delivery → verification → settlement. Each receipt settles once. Apply per-person limits within a published participation period and a total campaign budget, instead of unlimited rewards by weight. Correctly sorted small contributions should have reasonable eligibility conditions; splitting loads, adding water or producing more waste must not be encouraged.

Before opening, publish eligibility, materials, minimum conditions, settlement time, benefit quantities, claim process, expiry, redemption stock, any spending conditions and dispute support. Without funded, deliverable benefits, redeemable incentives stay closed.

How would duplicate or incorrect claims be handled?

Use a unique receipt, a versioned campaign rule and the minimum participation identifier needed to detect duplicates. Exclude container weight and rejected contamination. A user-entered quantity, photo or location alone is not recipient confirmation. Recovery must not erase the original food-waste record.

Duplicates, unusual load splitting and disputed handovers go to human review, not an automatic accusation of fraud. Show pending states and reasons, allow supporting information and appeals, and explain corrections to issued benefits without creating an unagreed cash debt.

What happens if collection or processing fails?

Pause new delivery prompts before capacity is exhausted. Explain rejection and suitable next steps; rejected material is not counted as collected. If accepted material is delayed, rerouted or cannot be regenerated, report its actual state and final destination.

Benefits follow the campaign’s pre-published eligible-delivery conditions. A downstream partner failure should not retroactively make a participant’s eligible delivery ineligible. Closing a campaign must explain deadlines, accepted batches and outstanding benefits.

Who would collect it and pay for it?

Proposed responsibilities: FreshLife provides records, rules and status feedback; collection points inspect and issue receipts; transport and processing partners document custody and treatment; sponsors fund published benefits. Actual partners, responsibilities and applicable conditions must be confirmed before opening.

There is no confirmed FreshLife collection network or processing partnership. The first stage focuses on local features without a paid recovery service. A physical pilot requires a funded plan for collection, transport, processing, exceptions and benefits. If the budget is missing, it does not open.

What data would be needed, and when is launch?

Planned local records remain user-managed, without precise location by default. Any future online verification or identity link must explain minimum data, recipients, retention and deletion before users choose to participate. Complete household inventories would not be shared with processors.

iOS, Android and HarmonyOS share the design rules; only an interactive prototype is complete. Native implementation and testing come before an availability claim. Collection and points require separate partner, funding and operating validation. No pilot date, location or redemption offer is announced.

Regional reference: the Hong Kong EPD food-waste recycling schemes describe local collection and treatment. This does not imply FreshLife participation or that these arrangements apply elsewhere. Checked 11 September 2026.
